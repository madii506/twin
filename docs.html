TWIN binds two coins to one market maker. When one runs ahead of the other, the maker takes a little profit off the leader and buys the laggard, so the chart that's behind gets bid and the pair moves back together. Everything below is how it works, what it costs, who holds what, and where every number comes from.

01 overview

        
Most pairs of coins that ride the same narrative fight each other. Money rotates out of one and into the other, and the loser bleeds while the winner runs. TWIN turns that rotation into a mechanic: you pick two coins, TWIN creates one maker wallet that holds SOL and both coins, and a small engine watches the gap between the two charts.

        
The gap is measured from a sync point: the two prices at the moment the maker went live. As long as both coins move the same amount, the gap is zero and the maker does nothing. When one coin pulls ahead by more than your band, the maker trims a slice of the leader and uses SOL to buy the laggard. Each further band of gap is a new step and triggers one more trade. When the gap closes, the ladder resets.

        
  coin A ──┐                                    ┌── trim the leader
           ├──▶ one maker wallet ──▶ the gap ──▶┤
  coin B ──┘    (SOL + A + B)       vs band     └── buy the laggard

        
    
          
  • what you picktwo pump.fun coins, a band, a buy size, a trim size
    • 
          
  • what TWIN createsone maker wallet per twin, used for nothing else
    • 
          
  • what moves moneyJupiter swaps signed by the maker wallet
    • 
          
  • who can stop itthe owner wallet: pause, change rules, withdraw everything
    • 
        

      


      

        
02 quick start

        
    
          
  1. pick two coins. Use Scan to grab two coins moving on pump.fun right now, paste two contract addresses into the bar at the top, or let Find rank which coins move most like one you already hold.
    2. 
          
  2. sync them in the lab. The Sync Lab loads real candles for both, shows how often they move together, and replays your rules over the last day so you see how many trades the maker would have made.
    3. 
          
  3. set the rules. Band, buy, trim, cooldown, max trade and price impact. Every rule has a hard range, listed in rules.
    4. 
          
  4. fund the maker. Connect a wallet, sign one message to prove it's yours, then send SOL to the new maker wallet. At least 0.05 SOL starts it; it never puts more than 5 SOL to work.
    5. 
          
  5. watch it, or walk away. The twin's page shows every pass, trade and transaction. You can pause, change rules, reset the sync point, add SOL, or withdraw everything back to your wallet at any time.

03 the gap and the band

        
Both coins are measured against their own price at the sync point, so a $0.0004 coin and a $0.02 coin are compared fairly. The gap is the log of one move divided by the other:

        
gap  = ln( (priceA / anchorA) / (priceB / anchorB) )
step = floor( |gap| / band )

        
A positive gap means A is ahead, a negative gap means B is ahead. Log makes it symmetric: A doubling while B stands still is the same size of gap as B halving while A stands still. With the default band of 8%, a gap of 9% is step 1, 17% is step 2, 25% is step 3.

        
 gap
 +24% ┤· · · · · · · · · · · ·  step 3 ─ trim A · buy B
 +16% ┤· · · · · · · · · · · ·  step 2 ─ trim A · buy B
  +8% ┤· · · · · · · · · · · ·  step 1 ─ trim A · buy B
   0% ┼──────────────────────── in sync · do nothing · ladder resets
  -8% ┤· · · · · · · · · · · ·  step 1 ─ trim B · buy A
 -16% ┤· · · · · · · · · · · ·  step 2 ─ trim B · buy A

        
The maker acts once per step, not once per pass. It remembers the highest step it already traded (the ladder) and only trades again when the gap opens a new one. If the gap shrinks by two steps or more, the ladder steps down so a fresh push can trigger again. If the gap falls back inside the band, the ladder resets to zero.

05 the engine pass

        
Every live twin gets a pass, then waits its cooldown before the next one. A pass is short and always runs in the same order:

        
pass(twin)
 ├─ prices     Jupiter price v3 · $A, $B and SOL in USD
 ├─ holdings   maker SOL + both token accounts, read from Solana RPC
 ├─ sample     price A, price B, gap, SOL and value saved for the chart
 ├─ gap        ln((pA/a0)/(pB/b0)) → step = floor(|gap|/band)
 ├─ step > ladder ?
 │   ├─ trim   sell trim% of the leader's bag (if it's worth more than $0.50)
 │   ├─ buy    min(max trade, (min(SOL, cap) − reserve) × buy%) of the laggard
 │   └─ ladder = step
 ├─ step = 0 ?        ladder → 0
 ├─ step < ladder−1 ? ladder → step + 1
 └─ next pass  now + cooldown minutes

        
Every swap is a Jupiter quote first. If the quote's price impact is above your limit, the trade is skipped and logged instead of forced. If there's no route, that's logged too. Buys under 0.004 SOL are skipped, they'd be mostly fees.

        
Who runs passes: a clock calls /api/tick roughly every 30 seconds, and every visit to the site nudges it as well. Each tick picks up to three due twins with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, so two ticks never run the same twin at once.

      


      

        
06 rules

        
Every rule is clamped to its range on the server, so a bad value can't slip through. The owner can change them at any time from the twin's page; the change applies from the next pass and is written to the twin's log.

        

          
ruledefaultrangewhat it does

          
band8%2–40%gap per step. Smaller trades more often, bigger waits for real divergence.

          
buy20%5–60%share of free SOL spent on the laggard per step.

          
trim35%0–100%share of the leader's bag sold per step. 0 means only ever buy.

          
cooldown3m1–120mminutes between passes.

          
max trade0.5 SOL0.01–5 SOLceiling on any single buy.

          
price impact4%0.5–10%skip a swap whose quote moves the price more than this.

          
slippage300 bps50–1500 bpshow far the fill may drift from the quote.

        

        
// fixed: min fund 0.05 SOL · cap 5 SOL put to work · 0.012 SOL kept for fees and rent · coins need ≥ $3000 liquidity

      


      

        
07 custody and your money

        
Read this one twice. To trade while you sleep, the maker has to sign its own swaps, so the server creates the maker's keypair and keeps it. That is custody: TWIN holds the key to the maker wallet, and you hold the key to the owner wallet that controls it.

        
    
          
  • maker keygenerated on the server, sealed with AES-256-GCM before it touches the database, opened only inside a pass
    • 
          
  • owner proofyou sign a one-time message with your wallet; the server checks the ed25519 signature and gives you a session
    • 
          
  • owner-onlyrules, pause, reset sync point, withdraw. Anyone else sees the twin, never the controls
    • 
          
  • where SOL can gointo Jupiter swaps for coin A or B, and back to the owner wallet. There is no other transfer in the code
    • 
          
  • your own walletnever shared. You sign the funding transfer yourself
    • 
        

        
Withdraw is one button. The twin switches to closing, and the next passes sell all of A, then all of B (with wider limits so it can get out), give the empty token accounts' rent back, and send every remaining lamport to the owner wallet, minus the network fee for that transfer. Then the twin is closed for good.

        
 owner wallet ──fund──▶ maker wallet ──swap──▶ Jupiter ⇄ $A / $B
      ▲                      │
      └──────── withdraw ────┘ sell A → sell B → close empty accounts → send all SOL

      


      

        
08 lifecycle

        
 [pending] ──fund ≥ 0.05 SOL──▶ [live] ◀──pause / resume──▶ [paused]
     │                              │
     └──────────── withdraw ────────┤
                                    ▼
                               [closing] ──coins sold, SOL sent──▶ [closed]

        
    
          
  • pendingmaker wallet exists, waiting for SOL. Funding sets the sync point to that moment's prices
    • 
          
  • livepasses run on the cooldown, trades happen on new steps
    • 
          
  • pausedpasses still sample prices for the chart, but never trade
    • 
          
  • reset syncsets both anchors to today's prices and the ladder to 0. Use it after a coin re-prices for good
    • 
          
  • closing → closedeverything sold and sent home. Final

09 sync lab and replay

        
The lab pulls real 5m, 15m or 1h candles for both coins from GeckoTerminal, lines them up on the same timestamps, and rebases both to 100 so you see who ran ahead. The lower chart is the gap with your band drawn on it; drag the band or move a slider and the replay reruns.

        
    
          
  • sync %correlation of the two coins' candle-to-candle log returns. 100% moves together, 0% is unrelated
    • 
          
  • replaythe same ladder rule over those candles, starting from 100 in cash, 0.5% fee per swap
    • 
          
  • compared toholding 50/50 from the first candle
    • 
          
  • not includedprice impact, failed routes, and how the maker's own buys move a thin chart
    • 
        

        
Replay shows how the rule behaves on a past day. It is not a promise about tomorrow.

      


      

        
10 find a twin

        
Paste one coin. TWIN takes up to nine Solana coins that people are boosting or profiling on Dexscreener right now, pulls 15m candles for each, lines each one up against yours, and ranks them by sync %. The top six come back with a small chart of both. Results are cached for five minutes per coin.

14 risks

        
    
          
  • you can lose money. Buying the laggard is a bet that it catches up. Sometimes the laggard is behind because it's dying, and the maker keeps buying it, one step at a time, until the SOL runs out or you stop it.
    • 
          
  • meme coins can go to zero. A rug or a dead dev takes the maker's bag of that coin with it.
    • 
          
  • custody. The server holds the maker key. A bug or a breach of the server could lose what's in the maker. Fund only what you can lose; it never puts more than 5 SOL to work.
    • 
          
  • thin liquidity. On small charts the maker's own buys move the price, and the impact limit may skip trades you expected.
    • 
          
  • missed passes. If prices or routes fail, a pass skips and logs it. Fast moves can open several steps between two passes; the maker trades once when it sees the new step.
    • 
          
  • not advice. TWIN is a tool. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy any coin.
